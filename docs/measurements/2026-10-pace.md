# Each ATS's pace, measured to its ceiling (#82)

Research for #82, run on the development machine on 2026-10-22, against the slugs of the
`classified-2026-10-22-lever` snapshot: how fast Greenhouse, Ashby and Lever serve probes and
sweeps without refusing, and the settings a run uses from that. Workable and Workday are researched
the same way once they are held (#80, #81). The full-scale runs at these settings come later in
this file.

## The bursts

A Python script sent each burst from the dev machine: a fixed number of workers, each holding
one kept-alive HTTPS connection, sending the app's user agent and no `Accept-Encoding`, as the
app's `HttpClient` does. Each burst asked the paths the adapters ask: Greenhouse's
`/v1/boards/<slug>/jobs` for a probe, plus `?content=true&pay_transparency=true` for a sweep;
Ashby's `/posting-api/job-board/<slug>?includeCompensation=true` for both; Lever's
`/v0/postings/<slug>?mode=json&limit=1` for a probe and `?mode=json` for a sweep. Slugs were drawn
at random from the snapshot (`setseed(0.82)` to `(0.88)`): probes over every company of the ATS,
sweeps over the `ACTIVE` ones, Lever's US and EU apart. No slug was asked twice in any of the
bursts, so Ashby's cache served none of them.

A ramp asked 1, 4, 8, 16 and then 32 at a time, with 30 s between bursts. Probe bursts were
`max(200, 40 × concurrency)` requests and sweep bursts `max(150, 20 × concurrency)`. A ramp
stopped going up at the first refusal, or when the median answer passed twice the
one-at-a-time median, or when the rate grew less than 20% over the level before.

### No ATS refused, at any level

None of the 12 180 requests of the ramps was refused: no `429`, `403` or `503`, and no challenge
page. Lever answered one `502`. So the ceiling of no ATS was found: what stopped the ramps was
the client.

- **The sweeps hit this machine's link.** All three ATSs' sweeps flattened at 6 to 8 MB/s,
  whichever ATS it was, and their answers slowed as the levels went up. So that is the link, not
  any ATS. Greenhouse's and Lever's sweeps were stopped at 32 for slowing down, Ashby's for its
  rate falling.
- **Greenhouse's probe grew in step with every level**, with no sign of slowing at 32. Its
  ceiling is above 136 a second.
- **Ashby's probe downloads whole boards**, since Ashby has nothing lighter: at 32 it drew
  9.7 MB/s and its answers started to slow. So it is near the link too.
- **Lever's median answer is a quarter of a second.** The slowness measured in
  `2026-10-lever.md` is a tail: one board in twenty takes 2 to 8 s, and the slowest up to a
  minute. So many requests at a time are what speeds Lever up. Its rate at 16 (12 a second) and
  at 32 (33 a second) follows how long that tail ran in each burst.

  and `504` but not `500`, so this board would have counted as unreadable. Of the 7012 requests to
  Lever in this file, two answered a gateway error, one at 16 at a time in a burst and one in the
  sustained run. `2026-10-lever.md` saw one at four at a time. Nothing ties them to load.

### What a refusal looks like

None was seen, so it is not known from a test. What is known:

- Lever documents a `429` for application `POST`s (source).
- Greenhouse's WAF and Ashby's Cloudflare would answer whatever their rules say. The readers
  already treat `429` and `503` as asking to be given time, and that stays the signal.
- No ATS sends `Retry-After` on its ordinary answers (test). Whether it sends one on a refusal is
  unknown, so a run honours it when it is there.

How long a refusal lasts is unknown for the same reason. The ramp's step of polling a fresh slug
every 5 s after a refusal was never reached.

## Bandwidth and the database

- **The link: 6 to 8 MB/s.** That is what every ATS's sweep flattened at, so it caps a sweep
  before the ATS does. Average board sizes from the 32-at-a-time sweeps: Greenhouse 0.37 MB,
  Ashby 0.26 MB, Lever 0.53 MB. All three ATSs swept side by side share the one link.
- **`VacancyStore.mirror`: not a cap.** Measured with a temporary test against the dev database
  on 300 Greenhouse boards read beforehand. The test was not committed, and those boards' vacancies
  are now as they read on 2026-10-22.
  - One after another: 149 boards, 5242 vacancies, in 1.1 s. That is 133 boards a second, with
    3 ms at the median, 27 ms at the 95th percentile and 127 ms at most.
  - Eight threads: 150 boards, 5148 vacancies, in 0.39 s, or 389 boards a second.
  - These boards were held and mostly unchanged, which is what a re-sweep meets. A board seen for
    the first time inserts every row, which is slower; that was not measured, since it would mean
    deleting held vacancies. A run that mirrors each board on the thread that read it, eight at a
    time, could be ten times slower than this and still mirror 39 boards a second, above every
    sweep setting below.

## The settings

**The rule.** A run starts each ATS's requests no faster than half the highest rate served
without a refusal. It keeps no more than half the number of requests open at once at which that
rate was served. There are two departures from it:

- **Sweeps.** No sweep found its ATS's ceiling, only the link's. Half the link-capped rate keeps a
  sweep of one ATS to about half the link.
- **Lever's probe.** It holds 16 requests open at once, not the 8 that half its 32 would give.
  Lever's own tail, not the rate, is what binds it, and 16 at a time drew nothing over the
  sustained 1500 requests.

| | Highest rate without a refusal | Requests open at once | Starts a second, at most | Expected run, against the current one |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Greenhouse probe | 136/s, 32 at a time | 16 | 60 | about 5 min for 18 925 companies, against 85m 50s |
| Greenhouse sweep | 22.7/s, 32 at a time (the link) | 8 | 10 | about 12 min for 7032 boards, about 3.7 MB/s, against 56m 43s |
| Ashby probe | 86.6/s, 32 at a time | 16 | 40 | about 3.5 min for 7920 companies, about 4 MB/s, against 35m 37s |
| Ashby sweep | 39.1/s, 16 at a time (the link) | 8 | 20 | about 3.5 min for 3737 boards, about 5 MB/s, against 22m 41s |
| Lever probe, each host | 71.8/s, 32 at a time | 16 | 30 | about 10 min for 8895 companies, held by its tail near 16/s, against 2h 17m |
| Lever sweep, each host | 18.4/s, 16 at a time (the link) | 8 | 9 | about 6 min for 1935 boards, about 4.8 MB/s, against 49m 43s |

Lever's limits are set per host. Its EU host has only 172 companies, so it finishes inside the US
host's run either way.

Several ATSs can run side by side, since they share no limit; their sweeps then share the link.
All three sweeps at once would ask about 13.5 MB/s of a 6 to 8 MB/s link. That slows each one
down, and no ATS refuses for it. On the production server the link is different, and these
settings stay under the ATSs' rates whatever the link is.

### When a run is refused anyway

The readers already treat `429` and `503` as a refusal, which `2026-10-lever.md` extends to
Lever's gateway errors. Under the new settings a run does four things:

1. **Retries the refused board.** It waits as long as `Retry-After` says when the ATS sends one,
   and otherwise for the back-off the reader has now: 1 s, doubling. The attempts stay the
   reader's five.
2. **Slows that ATS down for the rest of the run.** Each refusal halves both its starts a second
   and its requests open at once, down to one at a time and one a second. Other ATSs are not
   affected. A run never speeds back up: the next run starts from the settings again.
3. **Counts a board still refused after its retries as unreadable**, as now. The company keeps
   what it had.
4. **Treats Lever's `500` as a gateway error**, like `502` and `504`: retried without slowing the
   run, since nothing ties them to load. Lever's two readers get `500` added to what they
   retry.

A refusal during the full-scale check lowers that ATS's settings in the code, to the level the
run slowed to, and this file records why. A refusal in a later, ordinary run is only logged. A
pattern of them is a reason to measure again.
